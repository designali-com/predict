## What it measures

Serum calcium divided by serum phosphorus, both in mg·dL⁻¹. Bone needs both minerals in balance, and hormones such as PTH and vitamin D keep them there.

## Reference range

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Abbreviation | Ca/P ratio |
| Group | Bone and mineral |
| Unit | Ratio, no unit |
| Healthy range | 2–2.8 |
| Mean | 2.4 |
| SD | 0.2 |
| Direction | Normal best — either side is a concern. |

The mean is `(low + high) / 2` and the SD is `(high − low) / 4`, so the healthy range covers Z from −2 to +2.

## Reading the result

- **High** — Can point to high calcium or low phosphorus, as with an overactive parathyroid gland.
- **Low** — Can point to low calcium or high phosphorus, as with kidney disease or low vitamin D.

## Notes

- The middle of the range is best, so both sides are flagged.
- As a ratio, it has no unit. Look at calcium and phosphorus separately too.
- This page is general information, not medical advice. Discuss results with a clinician.
