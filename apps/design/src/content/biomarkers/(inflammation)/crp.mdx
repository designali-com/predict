## What it measures

C-reactive protein is an acute-phase protein made by the liver. It rises within hours of infection, injury, or an inflammatory flare and falls as it settles.

## Reference range

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Abbreviation | CRP |
| Group | Inflammation |
| Unit | mg·L⁻¹ |
| Healthy range | 0.1–4.9 |
| Mean | 2.5 |
| SD | 1.2 |
| Direction | ↑ Bad — a raised value is the concern. |

The mean is `(low + high) / 2` and the SD is `(high − low) / 4`, so the healthy range covers Z from −2 to +2.

## Reading the result

- **High** — Suggests inflammation somewhere in the body, from infection, injury, or an inflammatory condition. It does not say where.
- **Low** — Not a concern.

## Notes

- The standard test is less sensitive than hs-CRP at low levels. Use hs-CRP for cardiovascular risk.
- A single value is a snapshot. Trends over time say more.
- This page is general information, not medical advice. Discuss results with a clinician.
