## What it measures

Worked out from a routine full blood count: the neutrophil count divided by the lymphocyte count. Neutrophils rise with acute stress and inflammation, while lymphocytes often fall.

## Reference range

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Abbreviation | NLR |
| Group | Inflammation |
| Unit | Ratio, no unit |
| Healthy range | 1–3 |
| Mean | 2 |
| SD | 0.5 |
| Direction | ↑ Bad — a raised value is the concern. |

The mean is `(low + high) / 2` and the SD is `(high − low) / 4`, so the healthy range covers Z from −2 to +2.

## Reading the result

- **High** — Suggests systemic inflammation or physiological stress. It is linked to worse outcomes in heart disease, infection, and cancer.
- **Low** — Not a concern in this panel. Very low values can occur with some viral infections or low neutrophil counts.

## Notes

- Recent exercise, steroids, and acute illness can change it quickly.
- As a ratio, it has no unit.
- This page is general information, not medical advice. Discuss results with a clinician.
