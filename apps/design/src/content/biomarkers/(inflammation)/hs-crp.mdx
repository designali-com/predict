## What it measures

C-reactive protein is made by the liver when the body is inflamed. The high-sensitivity test measures it at very low levels, which picks up the slow, low-grade inflammation linked to heart and blood vessel disease.

The mean is `(low + high) / 2` and the SD is `(high − low) / 4`, so the healthy range covers Z from −2 to +2.

## Reading the result

- **High** — Points to ongoing low-grade inflammation and a higher cardiovascular risk. A short-term infection, injury, or hard exercise can also raise it.
- **Low** — Not a concern. Low values mean little inflammation.

## Notes

- Values above 10 mg·L⁻¹ usually mean an acute infection or injury, not long-term risk. Repeat the test once you are well.
- Read it alongside lipids and blood pressure, not on its own.
- This page is general information, not medical advice. Discuss results with a clinician.
