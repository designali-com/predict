## What it measures

Blood urea nitrogen comes from protein broken down in the liver and is cleared by the kidneys.

## Reference range

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Abbreviation | BUN |
| Group | Kidney and protein |
| Unit | mg·dL⁻¹ |
| Healthy range | 7–20 |
| Mean | 13.5 |
| SD | 3.25 |
| Direction | ↑ Bad — a raised value is the concern. |

The mean is `(low + high) / 2` and the SD is `(high − low) / 4`, so the healthy range covers Z from −2 to +2.

## Reading the result

- **High** — Can mean reduced kidney function, dehydration, a high-protein diet, or bleeding in the gut.
- **Low** — Not the concern this panel tracks. Low values can come from a low-protein diet or liver disease.

## Notes

- The BUN-to-creatinine ratio helps tell kidney causes from others.
- This page is general information, not medical advice. Discuss results with a clinician.
